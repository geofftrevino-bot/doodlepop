# Fix-It Farm — PROP RULES

> **Channel:** DoodlePop Kids · **Show:** Fix-It Farm
> Companion to **Character Bible v3**

---

## THE PRINCIPLE

**A prop that's always there stops being a prop.** It becomes costume — like a
hat — and the audience stops seeing it. Sprocket holding a wrench in every
single shot means picking up the wrench can never be a moment.

Props should appear **when they're used** and be absent otherwise, so that
reaching for one is a visible beat a child notices.

This already caused a real problem: in Short 10, Clover reclaims a carrot as
the punchline while visibly *already having one* in her pocket. The joke
survived, but only just.

**The tool belt is not a prop.** Sprocket's belt, with its red screwdriver and
blue-handled tool, is permanent costume and stays in every shot. Only the
**held** wrench is a prop.

Same for Rocco's straw hat and Clover's green overalls — costume, always
present.

---

## WHEN TO USE WHICH

### Use the **prop-free** version

- Greeting, sign-off, or talking straight to camera
- Walking, reacting, watching, listening
- Standing in a group or establishing shot
- **About to pick the prop up** — so the pickup is visible
- Holding something *else* (a carrot, a box, a bucket)
- Any shot where the prop would just be dangling unused

### Use the **prop** version

- Actively using it — turning, tightening, digging, watering
- The prop is the subject: "what does this do?"
- Mid-task with hands on the object
- The prop is the punchline

---

## THE ENGAGEMENT LINK

This ties directly to the **Scenery & Engagement Standard**. A prop should
support the engagement, not float beside it:

- Sprocket **fixing the tractor** → wrench version, and the wrench is **on the
  bolt**, not held up in the air
- Sprocket **explaining a rule** → prop-free, hooves raised, nothing in them
- Clover **planting** → trowel in the soil, not waved about
- Clover **naming colours** → prop-free, a free paw to point with

**The test:** if the prop isn't touching anything or being demonstrated, it
shouldn't be in the shot.

---

## PROMPT LANGUAGE

**With prop** — say what it's doing, not just that it exists:
> "…fits the silver wrench onto the bolt and gives it one firm turn"

**Prop-free** — say the hands are empty, or the generator will add something:
> "…both hooves empty and open, holding nothing"

Add to the negative prompt for prop-free shots:
> `holding a wrench, holding a tool, holding a carrot, object in hand`
